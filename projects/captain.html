What was I working on?
// UX DESIGNER

Captain ToonHead is a VR tower defense shooter where players actively defend their base while managing multiple incoming threats.

I designed and implemented the Sector Room and alert system for Survival Mode — a control layer that helps players understand enemy pressure across different sectors while staying engaged in moment-to-moment combat.

SECTOR ROOM DESIGN PROCESS
WHAT WAS THE CHALLENGE?

Survival Mode generates sectors procedurally, gradually expanding from one to multiple active zones with independent enemy waves, paths, and threats.

The challenge was to give players clear awareness of what was happening across sectors, while designing a VR-friendly interaction that allowed them to quickly assess danger and move between areas without breaking immersion or flow.

EXPLORATION & PROTOTYPING
WRIST INTERACTION
Explored a physical wrist interaction, allowing players to press a button directly in VR rather than relying on a traditional input.
The wrist became a natural point of access for sector information, designed to feel intuitive within the player's movement.
Alerts were introduced on the wrist to turn it into a reference point players could check during gameplay.
However, players often missed these alerts while focused on combat, revealing that this alone was not enough to maintain awareness.
// CONCEPT V6
ALERT SYSTEM
// 01
Extended to the HUD
Alerts were extended to the HUD to increase visibility and ensure players could recognize sector changes in real time.
// 02
Always within view
This allowed critical information to stay within the player's field of view without requiring additional actions.
// 03
Inform, not navigate
The system was designed to inform, not to navigate — keeping decision-making in the player's control.
SECTOR ROOM INTERFACE
The Sector Room was introduced as a dedicated space where players could step out of combat and assess the state of the game.
It acts as a central hub to understand multiple sectors without the pressure of real-time action.
Players can view each sector's layout, enemy types, and incoming waves, allowing them to evaluate threats at a glance.
From this space, players can choose a sector and instantly teleport, connecting awareness directly with action.
FINAL DESIGN
LEARNINGS
[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER ]
Spatial awareness is a design problem
In VR, players can't rely on peripheral vision the way they can on a flat screen. Designing clear sectoral cues taught me how physical presence changes the rules of information hierarchy.
Alerts need to guide, not interrupt
The biggest challenge was creating urgency without breaking flow. An effective alert system informs without pulling the player out of the experience.
